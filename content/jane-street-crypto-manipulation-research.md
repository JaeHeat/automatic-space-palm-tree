# Was Jane Street Manipulating Crypto Prices? — Research Brief

> **Content brief for a trading/finance audience.** Researched June 2026. This is a
> *balanced* breakdown: it separates what is **documented and proven** from what is
> **alleged but unproven** from what is **pure conspiracy**. Use it as a script
> backbone, not as a set of claims to assert on camera. Where something is unproven,
> say so — that credibility is the whole value of the piece.

---

## TL;DR (the one-paragraph version)

There are really **three different "Jane Street manipulated crypto" stories**, and they
get blended together online into one super-villain narrative. Keeping them separate is
the entire value of your content:

1. **The "10 AM slam" Bitcoin conspiracy (Feb 2026)** — viral on Crypto Twitter,
   **no firm evidence**, and credibly debunked by data. *Speculation.*
2. **The Terraform/UST insider-trading lawsuit (Feb 2026)** — a real lawsuit filed by
   Terraform's bankruptcy estate over front-running the 2022 UST de-peg. **Unproven
   allegation, actively contested in court.** *Serious but undecided.*
3. **The SEBI India index-manipulation case (Jul 2025)** — a regulator found
   prima-facie manipulation, impounded ~$566M, Jane Street paid into escrow. **This one
   is documented and real** — but it's *equities/derivatives in India, not crypto.*

The honest thesis for your content: **Jane Street has a proven willingness to push
right up to (and, per one regulator, past) the line in traditional markets — which is
exactly why the crypto accusations are plausible enough to go viral — but the specific
crypto-manipulation claims range from "unproven in court" to "debunked by data."**

## Part 1 — The Bitcoin "10 AM Slam" conspiracy (the viral one)

**What's alleged:** Starting around November 2025, retail traders on X claimed Jane
Street systematically dumped Bitcoin within minutes of the 10:00 a.m. ET equity open to
suppress the price, while holding short positions and/or buying spot Bitcoin ETF shares
(notably BlackRock's IBIT) at the resulting discount. The viral version claimed this
scheme drove BTC from ~$125,000 down to ~$62,000, and that one crypto entrepreneur put
the alleged profit at "$7 billion."

**Evidence accusers cite:**
- Repeated 2–3% BTC drops near the 10 a.m. ET open.
- Jane Street's sizable IBIT position (rumored "$2.5B+", later shown to be ~$790M in Q4
  2025 filings).
- Timing: BTC "stopped falling" right as Jane Street faced legal scrutiny.
- The "they already got caught in India" precedent (see Part 3).

**Why skeptics — including non-fans of Jane Street — don't buy it:**
- **The data doesn't hold up.** Crypto economist **Alex Kruger** found that since Jan 1,
  IBIT's cumulative return in the 10:00–10:30 window was **+0.9%** (the 10:00–10:15
  window was about –1%), and the pattern "closely mirrors Nasdaq performance" — i.e.
  broad market repricing, not a targeted BTC slam.
- **Dragonfly Capital partner Rob Hadick:** the argument "makes zero sense and
  completely misunderstands how derivatives and perps/futures work," calling it a search
  for a "boogeyman to blame for why they haven't made more money."
- **Jane Street's spokesperson:** "absolutely ridiculous."
- **The legal mechanics are mundane.** As an Authorized Participant (AP), Jane Street can
  legally short ETF shares without normal borrow costs, hedge with futures instead of
  buying spot immediately, and source BTC via OTC. That *creates* temporary, visible
  downside pressure around the open — through legal market-making, not rule-breaking.

**The strongest counter-argument to the conspiracy — on-chain data:** The Feb 2026 BTC
weakness has a crowded list of sellers that has nothing to do with Jane Street:
- **Long-term holders distributed ~143,000 BTC in 30 days** — fastest pace since Aug 2025
  (Glassnode / CryptoQuant).
- **Spot BTC ETFs saw ~$3.8B of cumulative outflows** through mid-Feb 2026, with BlackRock
  and Fidelity leading redemptions on Feb 18.
- **Retail capitulation:** wallets <10 BTC net-sold ~$258.5M in a 24h window.
- Miner forced-selling under tighter financing + Dec 2025 tax-loss harvesting.
- Coinbase Premium Index negative through much of February (persistent US selling).

**Verdict for this segment: SPECULATION / largely DEBUNKED.** If you present this as
fact, you lose credibility. The *interesting* content angle is **why** a market-structure
artifact (AP/ETF mechanics + a genuinely ugly tape) gets pattern-matched into a
conspiracy.

## Part 2 — The Terraform / UST lawsuit (the serious, undecided one)

**This is the real legal jeopardy, and it's specific.**

- **Feb 2026:** the bankruptcy administrator winding down **Terraform Labs'** estate sued
  Jane Street in the **Southern District of New York** (case before **Judge Dale E. Ho**),
  alleging Jane Street "abused market relationships to rig the market in its favor."
- **The core factual claim:** In **May 2022**, within minutes of Terraform itself
  withdrawing ~**$150M (150M UST)** from a Curve liquidity pool, a wallet linked to Jane
  Street allegedly executed an **additional ~85M UST withdrawal** using non-public
  information — accelerating the **UST de-peg** and the Luna/Terra "death spiral" that
  erased ~$40B+ in value.
- **Apr 23, 2026:** Jane Street **filed a motion to dismiss**, flatly denying all
  allegations.

**Why this one matters more than the BTC conspiracy:** it's a concrete, dated, on-chain
sequence of transactions argued in court with discovery — not a vibes-based X thread. But
**it is an allegation, not a finding.** As of this research it is *contested and
undecided.* Note also the framing nuance: shorting a stablecoin you believe is
structurally broken is *not automatically* illegal — the alleged crime is the **use of
material non-public information**, not the bearish bet itself.

**Verdict: SERIOUS ALLEGATION, UNPROVEN, ACTIVE LITIGATION.** Phrase everything as
"alleges," "the estate claims," "Jane Street denies." Do not narrate it as settled.

## Part 3 — The SEBI India case (the documented one — but it's NOT crypto)

This is the **factual anchor** of the whole story and the reason the crypto theories get
traction. Be precise that it is **Indian equity-index derivatives, not crypto.**

- **Jul 3, 2025:** India's securities regulator **SEBI** issued an ex-parte interim order
  barring Jane Street Group entities from Indian markets and impounding alleged unlawful
  gains of **₹4,843.57 crore (~$566M / often rounded to ~$540M)**.
- **The alleged scheme:** intraday index manipulation + "marking the close" on **NIFTY**
  and **BANK NIFTY**, concentrated on **derivatives expiry days**, Jan 2023–May 2025.
- **The mechanic (the part your audience will find juicy):** a **two-pronged strategy** —
  one entity aggressively bought the underlying bank stocks to push the index up, while
  another arm held far larger **derivatives** positions that profited from the move; then
  positions were unwound, with the firm allegedly netting up to ~**$4B** in related
  profits over the period.
- **Aftermath:** Jane Street paid the impounded sum into **escrow (Jul 14, 2025)**,
  **resumed trading Jul 21, 2025**, and **appealed**. The case moved through the
  Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT) into 2026.

**Verdict: DOCUMENTED REGULATORY ACTION (interim, under appeal).** Real, sourced, and the
single most important credibility brick. Caveat on camera: it's an **interim** order being
appealed, and it's a **different asset class and jurisdiction** from the crypto claims.

## Part 4 — The Jane Street → FTX/Alameda DNA (the narrative glue)

This is the part that makes the story *feel* inevitable to an audience, and it's
**factually solid** — just don't overclaim causation.

- **Sam Bankman-Fried** began his finance career as a trader at **Jane Street Capital**.
- **Caroline Ellison** was also a Jane Street trader; SBF recruited her (Mar 2018) to
  trade crypto at **Alameda Research**, where she became CEO.
- Alameda/FTX collapsed in **Nov 2022**; Ellison pleaded guilty to multiple fraud/
  conspiracy counts and was sentenced to **2 years**; her testimony helped convict SBF.

**The honest framing:** Jane Street is a legendary quant-trading "academy" whose alumni
went on to build one of crypto's biggest frauds. That's a **culture/pedigree** story — it
shows Jane Street's playbook and people are deep in crypto's DNA. It is **not** evidence
that Jane Street itself directed FTX's fraud. Keep the line clean: *"shared DNA, not
shared crime."*

## Suggested content structure (hook → payoff)

1. **Cold open / hook:** "A trading firm got banned in India, sued over a $40B crypto
   collapse, and accused of crashing Bitcoin every morning at 10 AM. How much of it is
   real?" *(Promises the separation-of-fact-from-fiction payoff.)*
2. **Who is Jane Street** — quant market-maker, ETF Authorized Participant, why that role
   is central to every accusation.
3. **The proven one (India/SEBI)** — establish that they *have* been caught bending rules.
4. **The serious one (Terraform/UST)** — the lawsuit, the on-chain sequence, "alleged."
5. **The viral one (10 AM slam)** — present the claim, then dismantle it with Kruger's
   data + on-chain sellers. *This is your credibility moment.*
6. **The DNA angle (FTX/SBF)** — culture story, "shared DNA not shared crime."
7. **Payoff / thesis:** market structure + a firm with a real rap sheet = fertile ground
   for conspiracy, but extraordinary claims still need evidence. End on the open
   questions (Terraform trial, SAT appeal).

## Talking points that land well
- "Authorized Participant" is the keyword that unlocks the whole debate — explain it once,
  clearly, and the audience can evaluate the claims themselves.
- "Legal market-making can *look* exactly like manipulation on a 1-minute chart." Great
  myth-busting beat.
- Contrast: **India case = a regulator with subpoena power found something.** **Bitcoin
  case = anonymous X threads.** Different evidentiary universes.

## Things to NOT say (legal/credibility landmines)
- Don't state the UST front-running or the BTC "slam" as **fact** — both are unproven; one
  is in active litigation. Always "alleges/denies."
- Don't claim Jane Street "ran FTX" or directed its fraud — unsupported.
- Don't repeat the "$7B from Bitcoin" figure as if sourced — it's one person's assertion.
- The SEBI order is **interim** and **under appeal** — say so.
